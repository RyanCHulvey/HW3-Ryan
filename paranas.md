1.	Briefly describe one important lesson about modularity that the paper describes and that you think is still relevant today.

**My Response:** I believe the crucial important lesson learned from this paper is stated in the conclusion: "it is almost always incorrect to begin the decomposition of a system into modules on the basis of a flowchart." Rather than assigning modules tasks in a chronological order, assigning modules to cover difficulties in isolation from one another proves to be more efficient and productive. It is also stated: "in most cases, design decisions transcend time of execution" reinforcing this idea where following flowcharts are not effective.

2.	Computing has advanced significantly since this paper was written. Briefly describe one challenge (not necessarily with modularity) or perspective that, while realistic in 1972, does not apply today.

**My Response:** I would believe what has changed the most would be communication and how software engineers often approach working in the modern world. With applications like slack/discord and technology like smartphones, communicating with fellow team members has become far easier when, in 1972, responses were often far more delayed. Also, it is far more likely to work from home in the modern world where as in 1972, that was far less common. Overall, the problem of communicating with fellow team members has practically been solved with the introduction of new technologies.

3.	Have you used modularity or information hiding in a previous CS course? Briefly describe where and how. If not, describe how you would apply it to a past assignment.

**My Response:** In my opinion, it is exceedingly hard to not adopt a modular-based approach in some way when working alone and especially when working with others. For instance, when working on my online shopping interface using JavaScript and SQL, I found myself breaking the large project into smaller sections like the default screen, the login screen, and the shopping screen. This made the project far simpler to handle and I was able to, without much stress, produce a working project. In general, I believe modularity and information hiding can be useful even within a modern world. When dealing with something big, break it into smaller pieces.
